🏫 Solving Small School Problems

A fun speaking lesson for kids! (Ages 8-12 | Level A1-B1)

Today's Goals:

  • Use vocabulary related to daily school problems.
  • Ask for help and give simple advice.
  • Explain a problem clearly using "because" or "so".

🧠 The "3 Steps" to Solve Any Problem

Memorize this simple strategy before we start speaking!

1. Stop & Think 🤔
"What is wrong?"
➔
2. Ask for Help 🙋
"Can you help me?"
➔
3. Fix & Thank 🌟
"Thank you!"

2. Vocabulary 📚

Learn these new words! Click "Read Example" to see how to use them.


Problem ⚠️
Something that is hard or wrong.

"I have a problem. I can't find my book."

Solve 💡
To find an answer or fix something.

"We worked together to solve the math puzzle."

Advice 🗣️
An idea to help someone.

"My teacher gave me good advice about studying."

Forgot 🧠
When you do not remember something.

"Oh no! I forgot my homework at home."

Argue 😠
To speak angrily with someone.

"Friends sometimes argue, but they say sorry later."

Apologize 🤝
To say "I am sorry".

"I will apologize because I broke your pencil."

Share 🍕
To let someone use your things.

"I will share my snacks with you."

Compromise ⚖️
To agree by giving up a little bit.

"We both want the toy, so let's compromise and take turns."

🟢 Easy Questions

Q1. You forgot your pencil case. What do you do? ✏️

💡 Idea Bank (Click to open)
  • Ask a friend to borrow a pencil.
  • Ask the teacher for a spare pen.
  • Check your bag again carefully.
📝 Sample Answers
Short

I ask my friend, "Can I borrow a pencil, please?"

Extended

First, I look in my bag again. If it is not there, I tell my teacher that I forgot it. Then, I ask the teacher or a friend if I can borrow a pen for the day.

Q2. You spill water on your desk. What do you do? 💧

💡 Idea Bank
  • Get some paper towels.
  • Tell the teacher.
  • Move your books so they don't get wet.
📝 Sample Answers
Short

I get some paper towels to clean it up.

Extended

First, I quickly move my books so they stay dry. Then, I tell the teacher, "I spilled my water." I go to the bathroom to get paper towels and clean the desk.

Q3. You cannot open your locker or your box. What do you do? 🔐

💡 Idea Bank
  • Try one more time slowly.
  • Ask a tall friend to help.
  • Ask the teacher or the school staff.
📝 Sample Answers
Short

I ask my teacher, "Can you help me open this, please?"

Extended

I try to open it slowly one more time. If it is still stuck, I find my teacher. I say, "Excuse me, my box is stuck. Can you please help me open it?"

🟡 Medium Questions

Q4. Two friends want to play with the same basketball. How can they solve this? 🏀

💡 Idea Bank
  • Play a game together with the ball.
  • Take turns (5 minutes each).
  • Find a second basketball.
📝 Sample Answers
Short

They should play a game together and share the ball.

Extended

They shouldn't argue. A good idea is to play a basketball game together. If they want to play different games, they can take turns. One person plays for 10 minutes, and then the other person plays.

Q5. Someone is sitting in your favorite seat in the cafeteria. What do you do? 🪑

💡 Idea Bank
  • Sit somewhere else. It's okay!
  • Ask them if you can sit next to them.
  • Find another table with your friends.
📝 Sample Answers
Short

I just find a different seat. It is not a big problem.

Extended

I would politely say hello and find another chair. Or, if there is a space, I might ask, "Can I sit next to you?" It is fun to sit with new people sometimes.

Q6. You accidentally tear your friend's drawing. What do you do? 📄

💡 Idea Bank
  • Apologize immediately.
  • Use tape to fix it.
  • Help them draw a new one.
📝 Sample Answers
Short

I apologize and say, "I'm so sorry! Let me fix it."

Extended

First, I apologize quickly because I made a mistake. Then, I find some clear tape to fix the paper carefully. If it is ruined, I offer to help them draw a brand new picture.

🔴 Opinion Questions

Q7. Is it better to solve a problem yourself or ask a teacher? Why? 👩‍🏫

💡 Idea Bank
  • Small problems: Solve it yourself (like dropping a pencil).
  • Big problems: Ask a teacher (like if someone is hurt).
  • Teachers give good advice.
📝 Sample Answers
Short

I think it depends. Small problems I fix myself, but big problems need a teacher.

Extended

In my opinion, you should try to solve small problems yourself first. For example, if you forget a pen, you can just ask a friend. But if there is a big argument or someone is crying, you must ask a teacher because they know how to help safely.

Q8. Do you think saying "sorry" always solves the problem? 🗣️

💡 Idea Bank
  • Yes, it makes people feel better.
  • No, you also need to fix what you broke.
  • It is a good start, but actions matter more.
📝 Sample Answers
Short

I think saying sorry is important, but you also have to fix your mistake.

Extended

In my opinion, saying sorry is a great start because it shows you care. However, it doesn't always solve everything. If you break someone's toy, you say sorry, but you should also try to fix it or buy a new one. Actions are very important.

6. Role-Play Time! 🎭

Work with a partner. Choose a role and act out the situation!

🗣️ Useful Phrases for Role-Play

If you HAVE a problem... If you are HELPING...
"Excuse me, can you help me?" "What's wrong?" / "Are you okay?"
"I have a problem. I lost my..." "Don't worry. I can help you."
"I'm so sorry. I didn't mean to..." "It's okay. Let's fix it together."

Scenario A: The Lost Homework 📄

Situation: Student A lost their homework. They are looking everywhere. Student B wants to help them.

👦 Role A: You are worried. Explain the problem.

👧 Role B: Give advice and help them look.

💬 Dialogue Hints

A: Oh no! I can't find my...

B: What's wrong? Can I help you?

A: I lost my homework. I think I left it...

B: Don't worry. Why don't we check your...

Scenario B: The Playground Argument ⚽

Situation: It is break time. There is only one ball left.

👦 Role A: You really want to play Soccer.

👧 Role B: You really want to play Dodgeball.

💬 Dialogue Hints

A: I got the ball! Let's play soccer!

B: But I wanted to play dodgeball today!

A: We played dodgeball yesterday. It's my turn to choose.

B: Maybe we can compromise. What if we...
